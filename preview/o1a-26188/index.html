The night she found out she was invisible

Her husband was out on the grass with the guys he'd known since college. She never liked one woman in that crowd. She calls her Karen: thirty-two, practically lives at the gym, and touches every husband a little too long.

Out of nowhere, Karen came up behind him, pulled him close and kissed the side of his neck. She yelled "stop." The music swallowed it. Nobody heard her. Karen let go and danced backwards, smiling like she was nothing.

Later at the bar, a tall bartender spent five minutes flirting with her husband while she stood there waiting for their drinks. When the drink finally came, the bartender didn't even look at her.

2 a.m., in front of the mirror

She was still awake, replaying it. Thirty-eight. Ten years of marriage and stress. Puffy every morning, heavy by every afternoon, and a body that didn't feel like hers anymore.

"I wasn't mad at Karen anymore. I was mad at the woman in the mirror."

The call to Aunt Sophia

Aunt Sophia has lived in Brazil for fifteen years and runs a recovery studio for pro athletes. She listened to the whole story and said one thing: "I'm flying in Friday. Don't sign up for any confidence classes."

She got off the plane, held her niece by the shoulders and said, "You don't need to learn how to fight. You need to become the woman nobody tries that with."

What Aunt Sophia told her on day three

"You don't have a marriage body," Sophia said. "You have a stressed body. Ten years of holding your breath around him. Your gut has been out of balance for years, and when it is, everything feels heavy: the bloat by afternoon, the puffiness you see in the morning, the tired that sleep doesn't fix."

"Stress shows up in the gut first. So that's where we start."
